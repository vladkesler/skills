# ACS Sustainable Chemistry & Engineering: what the journal requires

Compiled from the journal's author guidelines (researcher-resources.acs.org/publish/author_guidelines?coden=ascecg, version dated 27 Aug 2026, with the June 2025 version for comparison), the ACS TOC-graphic guidelines (Feb 2024), ACS policy pages, and the journal's own editorials. The guidelines change; a fact marked *(2026 revision)* changed within the last year. When a requirement decides acceptance, tell the author to confirm against the live page at submission.

Journal facts: *ACS Sustainable Chem. Eng.* (CASSI abbreviation), coden ascecg, ISSN 2168-0485, Editor-in-Chief Peter Licence (Nottingham) since 2023; journal office admin@sustain.acs.org, EiC eic@sustain.acs.org.

## 1. Scope: what the editors mean by "sustainable"

Scope statement: the journal "publishes content that address challenges of sustainability in the chemical enterprise and advance principles of Green Chemistry and Green Engineering," with example topics "Green Chemistry, Green Manufacturing and Engineering, Biomass or Wastes as Resources, Alternative Energy, and Life-Cycle Assessment."

The editors' four "pillars" are novelty, quality, scope, and metrics, and their definition of novelty is the thing most authors miss (Moores et al., "Reintroducing the INTRODUCTION", *ACS Sustainable Chem. Eng.* 2024, 12, 8581):

> "Novelty here must be understood as being anchored in sustainability. Significant improvements in performance for any molecule, material, or process, where the sustainability aspect is not identified, do not meet the novelty threshold."

> "A molecule, material, or process is never 'green' in the absolute sense of the term, but it may be considered 'greener' when compared to what is already known. We suggest that, in the absence of comparative data, the inclusion of appropriate metrics or the presentation of an evidence-based narrative statement, the terms 'clean', 'green', or 'sustainable' are avoided. We also discourage the use of the terms 'green' or 'sustainable' in manuscript titles."

The guidelines' "Sustainability Metrics" paragraph asks authors to (i) place the work in the life cycle or supply chain of the product or process, even qualitatively; (ii) cite or describe persistence and toxicity data; (iii) use green-chemistry metrics (atom economy, mass efficiency, E-factor, carbon efficiency, emission rates); (iv) describe the calculation of any non-standard metric; and (v) show the metric in the TOC graphic.

Out of scope, stated explicitly by the editors:
- Performance improvements with no identified sustainability advantage; incremental advances; "the mere juxtaposition of two already reported successful strategies."
- Processes or products with **increased toxicity** relative to comparators, even if other metrics improve (Toxicity editorial, 2026). Toxic reagents, solvents, catalysts, or products must be disclosed and their unavoidability justified. Managing toxicity by containment or PPE instead of benign design is out of scope. Papers that only evaluate toxicity are out of scope.
- Pollutant **treatment** rather than pollution **prevention** (stated for Perspectives; the same framing runs through the scope).
- Biomass as a reactant does not by itself make a material sustainable; using renewable electricity to compensate for inefficiency does not either; biobased polymers made with toxic or more waste-intensive routes than the fossil counterpart do not meet expectations.
- Blending established materials with biomass, using well-known renewable monomers, or minor variations of known syntheses are "incremental" unless a compelling sustainability case is made (Sustainable Materials editorial, 2020).
- Papers that are only characterization, or a compilation of data from sophisticated tools without a scientific advance (Nanoscience editorial).
- Biocatalysis: unisolated products at low aqueous concentration measured only chromatographically are "premature"; "I use a biocatalyst, therefore my reaction is sustainable" is rejected reasoning.

Field-specific reporting expectations are in `sustainability-claims.md`.

## 2. Manuscript types and limits

| Type | Limit (word-equivalents) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Article | 7000 | Standard research paper. "Manuscripts that report data of a routine nature will not be accepted." |
| Letter | 3000 | Preliminary but original work meriting fast publication; sections optional. First author's status (doctoral student / postdoc) must be given in the cover letter for "emerging scholar" Letters. |
| Viewpoint | 1000 | By invitation only *(2026 revision)*. |
| Letter to the Editor | 500 | Within 18 months of the original paper. |
| Perspective | (10 000 in the 2025 guidelines) | **Not listed in the Aug 2026 guidelines.** If the user is writing one, tell them to confirm with admin@sustain.acs.org before investing effort. |

**Word-equivalent counting.** Everything except the title page, references, captions, and TOC graphic counts, including the abstract, acknowledgments, and SI description. Each small figure, scheme, or table adds 300; each large one adds 600 or more. A 6000-word article with four small figures is a 7200-word article and over the limit. Manuscripts over the limit may be returned; exceeding it needs a "compelling argument" in the cover letter. The guidelines note that "manuscript text can often be shortened by 10% with no loss of context," which is a useful editing target. Supporting Information is not counted, so moving material there is the standard fix.

## 3. Required elements, in manuscript order

1. **Title.** "Specific and informative"; "a 20-word title is too long"; generic name in parentheses after any trade name; no abbreviations. Editors discourage "green" and "sustainable" in titles. No "novel", "first", "facile".
2. **Authors and affiliations.** First name, middle initial(s), last name; no titles; affiliation where the work was done (present address under a "Present Address" note near the Acknowledgments). Corresponding author(s) marked with an asterisk, email on the first page, no more than three corresponding authors. ORCID iDs are **not** typed into the manuscript; they are linked in the ACS Publishing Center profile and required for all authors at revision.
3. **Abstract.** 150–200 words, unstructured, no reference numbers, abbreviations defined, "include major quantitative data if they can be stated briefly, but do not include background material." The 2026 Toxicity editorial expects an explicit, benchmarked sustainability-advance statement here.
4. **Keywords.** 5–8, ideally including terms not in the title; repeated in the cover letter.
5. **Main text**: Introduction, Experimental Section (or Materials and Methods), Results and Discussion (combined or separate), Conclusions (customary; editors expect the benchmarked sustainability statement repeated here). Sections are **not numbered**. No footnotes. Pages numbered. Figures, schemes, tables, and equations embedded at the point of relevance (ACS "Fast Format").
6. **Associated Content**: (a) **Supporting Information** paragraph: a non-sentence description of each file's contents with the file type. Sufficient: "Supporting Information: ¹H NMR spectra for all compounds (PDF)". Insufficient: "Figures S1–S3". (b) **Data Availability Statement** (ACS strongly encourages one; include it): "The data underlying this study are available in the published article and its Supporting Information" / "...openly available in [repository] at [DOI]" / "...not publicly available due to [reason]".
7. **Author Information**: Corresponding Author (name, email; asterisk); Present Address if any; Author Contributions (optional in the guidelines; "‡ These authors contributed equally" is the usual form; a CRediT sentence is welcome but not mandated); Notes: the conflict-of-interest statement, default "The authors declare no competing financial interest."
8. **Acknowledgments.** All funding sources for all authors (also entered in the Funder Registry at submission); meeting presentation if any; **generative-AI disclosure** ("when and how the tools were used"); optional citation-diversity or land statements; no political statements.
9. **References.** See §5.
10. **TOC/Abstract graphic** on the last page, labeled "For Table of Contents Use Only". See §6.
11. **Synopsis** *(2026 revision)*: a ~20-word, non-technical, single-sentence statement of how the paper relates to sustainability was **mandatory through the June 2025 guidelines and is absent from the Aug 2026 guidelines**. It is still what editors screen for, costs one sentence, and published papers carry it as a one-line "short abstract". Draft one from the paper's numbers, place it with the TOC graphic, and tell the author it is optional under the current guidelines and to check the live page.

## 4. Section-by-section expectations

**Introduction.** "No more than two double-spaced pages without figures or tables and fewer than 20 references." Cite the pertinent literature, not a survey; if a review exists, cite it rather than its sources; but the editors also ask authors to favor original articles over reviews when crediting specific advances, and to avoid excessive self-citation. Failing to cite important precedent "could be interpreted as a breach of the ACS Ethical Guidelines and is a reason for rejection without external review."

The editors' recommended five-part structure (2024 editorial), which is a good skeleton to edit toward:
1. Context: why the problem matters, briefly.
2. State of the art: the specific prior results with their numbers and citations.
3. Knowledge gap, stated quantitatively. "General qualitative characterizations such as 'all past syntheses use large amounts of solvents, high temperatures, and extended reaction times' are not useful and should be replaced by more quantitative and precise assessments." They suggest a benchmark table with sustainability metrics (main text or SI) and a scheme comparing benchmark and new system.
4. The original idea: "In this work, we..."
5. Implications: what the result enables, in proportion to the evidence.

**Experimental Section.** Pertinent and critical factors only; common procedures omitted; details to SI. Special reagents' preparation described. **Safety**: "Authors must emphasize any unexpected, new, and/or significant hazards or risks associated with the reported work" in this section (ACS Safety Statement Style Sheet gives wording). SI units; Imperial units followed by SI in parentheses. CAS/IUPAC names; CAS numbers suggested for unfamiliar compounds; trade names defined at first use. For computational work: force fields, basis sets, model equations, code and versions (cited).

**Results and Discussion.** "Compare your results with those of others. Be complete but concise. Avoid irrelevant comparisons or contrasts, speculations unsupported by the new information presented in the paper, and verbose discussion." Error and uncertainty analysis, and for computational work validation and sensitivity analysis, are listed by the editors among the reasons manuscripts are not sent to review.

**Conclusions.** Not mandated, but the editors look here for the benchmarked sustainability statement. Findings with numbers, the limitation, and what would make the result matter at scale.

**Tables.** One-phrase or one-sentence title; details in footnotes; Arabic numerals; built with the table feature, one entry per cell, no merged cells; supplement rather than duplicate text and figures.

**Equations.** Chemical equations balanced and numbered with Arabic numerals in one sequence with mathematical equations; mathematical arguments "as brief as possible"; formulas per the ACS Style Guide.

**Supporting Information.** Separate file(s), submitted with the manuscript, reviewed with it, published free with its own DOI. "Place tables and figures that augment the article but are otherwise unessential to the major themes in Supporting Information." Unpublished or in-press references cited in the paper must be supplied as SI for review.

## 5. References and citations

- Numbered with Arabic numerals in order of first citation, "as superscript closed up to the last character and outside any punctuation marks": `...as reported previously.¹²`
- One reference per number; **compound references are not permitted** (no "12(a), 12(b)").
- Titles are required. DOIs "highly preferred" wherever they exist.
- For initial submission, "references can be provided in any style, but they must be complete, including titles"; the journal applies its style at production. So a consistent, complete list in ACS format (`acs-style.md` §9) is the target, and a mixed-style list is a sign the author should run it through a reference manager before submission.
- Data and software must be cited in the reference list when used (ACS Research Data Policy format: `Author 1; ...; Author 10; et al. Title. *Database*, version. Publisher, date. DOI or URL`).

## 6. Graphics

- **TOC/Abstract graphic.** Mandatory for Articles and Letters; peer-reviewed with the paper. Journal size: **3.33 in. × 1.875 in. (8.47 × 4.76 cm; 240 × 135 pt)**. Sans-serif labels, ~8 pt, never below 6 pt. Simple, little text, color for impact; "tables or spectra are not acceptable"; avoid reusing a figure from the text. Must be the authors' own original artwork; nothing from websites even if public domain; no photos of people without releases; **AI-generated TOC graphics are prohibited** by ACS policy. Editors encourage showing the sustainability metric in it. Placed on the last page with the label "For Table of Contents Use Only". TIFF at 300 dpi (color) / 1200 dpi (line art), or EPS.
- **Figures.** Preferred embedded in the Word file; TIFF/PDF/EPS/CDX also accepted. Resolution: 1200 dpi line art, 600 dpi grayscale, 300 dpi RGB color (CMYK may shift). Single column up to 240 pt (3.33 in.) wide, preferred; double column 300–504 pt (4.167–7 in.); maximum depth 660 pt including caption. Lettering ≥ 4.5 pt at final size, Helvetica/Arial; lines ≥ 0.5 pt. Color is free, but "avoid relying on color alone" (symbols, labels, patterns; WCAG 4.5:1 text contrast). Multipart figures assembled into one graphic with part labels. Caption below the figure, understandable without the text; symbol keys preferably inside the artwork. Reused graphics need a credit line and written permission. Production does not enhance figures.
- **Schemes and charts** may carry a brief caption; structures drawn with ChemDraw or equivalent.
- **Cover art** (optional, at revision): 6.28 × 5.82 in., ≥ 300 dpi, caption under 50 words.

## 7. Policies that affect the text

- **Generative AI** (ACS policy, Dec 2024): AI tools cannot be authors; text or image generation must be disclosed in the Acknowledgments with when and how; substantial use in Methods; AI in figures disclosed in the caption; no AI TOC graphics; authors are responsible for accuracy including references; editors may reject if AI use is "too extensive" (AI-written commentary or literature review specifically named).
- **Preprints**: allowed (ChemRxiv, bioRxiv, arXiv); disclose with link in the cover letter and note what changed. Any other prior publication is forbidden.
- **Ethics**: iThenticate screening for plagiarism and self-plagiarism; single-journal submission; all coauthors must consent; authorship changes after acceptance are "generally not considered"; human/animal work needs ethics approval statements; hazards must be identified; dual-use disclosed.
- **Conflict of interest**: the corresponding author declares for all authors at submission; published in the paper; default statement "The authors declare no competing financial interest." Financial interests over $5000 or 5% equity, employment within three years, and non-financial conflicts count.
- **Research data**: sharing "strongly encouraged"; a Data Availability Statement in Associated Content; repositories should be persistent (≥ 5 years) and DOI-issuing; code must be described (language, version, OS) and made available on request.
- **Funding**: every source for every author, in the manuscript and the Funder Registry tool.
- **Review process**: editors screen every paper before external review; appeals within one month, one per manuscript, rarely successful; a rejected paper may be resubmitted only with editor permission and a point-by-point response.

## 8. Cover letter (mandatory contents)

The guidelines list what must be in it:
- A rationale for publication "particularly with respect to its originality, scientific merit, and environmental importance"; authors are "strongly encouraged to describe how the research demonstrates the Principles of Green Chemistry and/or the Principles of Green Engineering."
- Authors and affiliations; manuscript title; the 5–8 keywords.
- For Letters, whether the first author is a doctoral student or postdoc.
- Contact information for all authors.
- Preprint disclosure with link, if any.
- The argument for exceeding the word limit, if applicable.
- Previous submission details and manuscript IDs, if any, with changes made.
- Safety considerations should also be flagged to the editors.
Suggested reviewers are optional; avoid same-institution or conflicted names; institutional emails.

## 9. Why manuscripts are desk-rejected

From the guidelines: "insufficient novelty and/or originality; lack of sufficient relevance to the scope of the journal; failure to place the rationale for the study or the results in the context of existing literature; data that describe a case study or offer only an incremental advance in the scientific topic; insufficient quality of the data; and problems with manuscript presentation (for example, adherence to length limits, inadequate English, figure quality, etc.)."

From the editors' 2019 editorial "Why Wasn't My ACS Sustainable Chemistry & Engineering Manuscript Sent Out for Review?": "lack of explanations, mechanisms, or testable hypotheses for observed data; inadequate error and uncertainty analyses; inadequate validation or sensitivity analyses of computational work; failure to summarize how the contribution advances the current state of the field; and insufficiently clear language or organization."

From other editorials: violating a green-chemistry or green-engineering principle without qualitative or quantitative justification; failing to cite precedent; increased toxicity relative to comparators; sustainability claimed only in the cover letter and absent from abstract, results, and conclusions; "LCA-lite" claims without a functional unit or system boundary; a named baseline missing for the claimed metric.

Each of these maps to an editing action: quantify the gap in the Introduction, add the benchmark comparison, add error bars and n, hedge mechanism to the evidence, put the sustainability statement in the abstract and conclusions, name the baseline, and cut the length.

## 10. Submission checklist

- [ ] Article ≤ 7000 word-equivalents (figures/tables at 300–600 each); Letter ≤ 3000
- [ ] Title specific, under ~15 words, no "green/sustainable/novel/first", no abbreviations
- [ ] Abstract 150–200 words, no citations, abbreviations defined, key numbers and the benchmarked sustainability statement
- [ ] 5–8 keywords in manuscript and cover letter
- [ ] Sections unnumbered; no footnotes; pages numbered; graphics embedded at point of relevance
- [ ] Introduction ≤ 2 double-spaced pages, < 20 references, quantified gap, specific prior results
- [ ] Experimental section with safety/hazard statement; SI units; CAS/IUPAC names
- [ ] Results compared with prior work (table); error/uncertainty reported; mechanism tied to evidence
- [ ] At least one quantified sustainability metric benchmarked against the incumbent; toxicity disclosed
- [ ] Conclusions restate the benchmarked sustainability advance
- [ ] Associated Content: SI description with file types; Data Availability Statement
- [ ] Author Information: corresponding author with email and asterisk (≤ 3); contributions; COI statement
- [ ] Acknowledgments with all funding; AI-use disclosure if applicable
- [ ] References numbered in citation order, superscript, one per number, titles and DOIs
- [ ] TOC graphic 3.33 × 1.875 in., original, labeled, last page, not AI-generated; sustainability metric shown if possible
- [ ] (Optional under 2026 guidelines) one-sentence sustainability synopsis with the TOC graphic
- [ ] Supporting Information as a separate file; unpublished references supplied for review
- [ ] Cover letter with all §8 items, including the Green Chemistry / Green Engineering principles argument
- [ ] ORCID iDs linked in ACS profiles for all authors; preprint disclosed if posted
