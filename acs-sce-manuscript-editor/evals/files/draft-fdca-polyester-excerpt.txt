Title: Bio-based Poly(ethylene 2,5-furandicarboxylate) Copolyesters with Enhanced Toughness via Incorporation of Renewable Diols: A Novel Route towards Sustainable Packaging Materials

ABSTRACT: Poly(ethylene 2,5-furandicarboxylate) (PEF) is widely regarded as the most promising bio-based alternative to poly(ethylene terephthalate) (PET), owing to its superior gas barrier properties and 100% renewable origin. However, the inherent brittleness of PEF significantly hinders its practical applications in packaging. In this work, a series of novel PEF-based copolyesters were successfully synthesized by incorporating varying amounts of isosorbide and 1,4-butanediol as comonomers through a facile two-step melt polycondensation process. The chemical structure, thermal properties, mechanical properties and barrier properties of the copolyesters were systematically investigated. Remarkably, the copolyester containing 20 mol% 1,4-butanediol exhibited an elongation at break of 285%, which is more than 60 times higher than that of neat PEF, while maintaining a high glass transition temperature and excellent oxygen barrier properties. Furthermore, the copolyesters showed good compostability under industrial composting conditions. This study not only provides a novel strategy for toughening PEF but also demonstrates the great potential of these fully bio-based copolyesters as sustainable packaging materials, paving the way towards a circular plastics economy.

INTRODUCTION

Plastics have become an indispensable part of modern life, with global production exceeding 400 million tons annually. However, the vast majority of plastics are derived from fossil resources and are non-biodegradable, leading to severe environmental issues such as greenhouse gas emissions and plastic pollution. In this context, the development of bio-based and biodegradable polymers has attracted tremendous attention from both academia and industry as a promising solution to address these challenges.

2,5-Furandicarboxylic acid (FDCA) has been identified by the US Department of Energy as one of the top value-added chemicals from biomass. PEF, the polyester of FDCA and ethylene glycol, is considered the bio-based counterpart of PET. Compared to PET, PEF exhibits a higher glass transition temperature (Tg ≈ 85 °C vs. 76 °C), a lower melting temperature, and significantly enhanced barrier properties towards O2 (11 times), CO2 (19 times) and H2O (2 times). These outstanding properties make PEF an ideal candidate for the packaging of beverages and food. Nevertheless, PEF suffers from intrinsic brittleness, with an elongation at break of only about 4%, which severely limits its applications in flexible packaging and films.

To overcome this drawback, various strategies have been explored, including blending, plasticization and copolymerization. Among them, copolymerization is regarded as the most effective approach, since it enables the tuning of the properties at the molecular level. A variety of comonomers have been incorporated into PEF, such as 1,4-butanediol, 1,6-hexanediol, 1,4-cyclohexanedimethanol and isosorbide. For instance, Papageorgiou et al. synthesized poly(butylene furanoate) (PBF) and found it to be much more ductile than PEF. Wang et al. reported PEF-co-PBF copolyesters with elongation at break up to 600%. However, most of the reported comonomers are petroleum-based, which compromises the renewable nature of the resulting polymers. Moreover, the trade-off between toughness and barrier properties remains a significant challenge, as the incorporation of flexible comonomers usually leads to a dramatic decrease in Tg and barrier performance.

Herein, we report a novel series of fully bio-based PEF copolyesters incorporating isosorbide and 1,4-butanediol, both of which can be derived from renewable resources. Isosorbide, a rigid bicyclic diol derived from glucose, was introduced to maintain a high Tg, while the flexible 1,4-butanediol was employed to improve the toughness. The synergistic effect of these two comonomers enables the copolyesters to achieve an unprecedented balance of toughness, thermal stability and barrier properties. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first report on PEF copolyesters simultaneously incorporating isosorbide and 1,4-butanediol. Furthermore, the compostability of the copolyesters was evaluated to assess their end-of-life options. We believe this work will provide new insights into the design of high-performance bio-based polyesters for sustainable packaging applications.
