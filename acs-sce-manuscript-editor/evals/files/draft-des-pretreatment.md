Novel and Green Deep Eutectic Solvent Pretreatment of Corn Stover for Highly Efficient Lignin Extraction and Enhanced Enzymatic Saccharification: A Sustainable Approach

Abstract

In recent years, the valorization of lignocellulosic biomass has emerged as a crucial strategy in the realm of sustainable chemistry. Herein, we report for the first time a novel and green deep eutectic solvent (DES) system based on choline chloride and lactic acid for the highly efficient pretreatment of corn stover. The DES pretreatment was found to remove up to 78.4% of lignin at mild conditions, leading to a remarkable enhancement in glucose yield (from 21.3% to 84.7%) after enzymatic hydrolysis. Comprehensive characterization by FTIR, XRD, SEM and 2D-HSQC NMR was utilized in order to delve into the structural changes of the biomass and extracted lignin. Notably, the DES could be recycled for five cycles with negligible loss of performance, underscoring the sustainability of the process. This work paves the way for the development of green and sustainable biorefineries and holds great promise for the production of value-added chemicals from renewable resources [1-3].

1. Introduction

Lignocellulosic biomass is the most abundant renewable resource on earth and plays a pivotal role in the transition towards a sustainable bioeconomy. It is widely believed that the effective utilization of lignocellulose is crucial for reducing our dependence on fossil fuels. However, the intricate and recalcitrant structure of lignocellulose, comprised of cellulose, hemicellulose and lignin, poses a significant challenge to its efficient conversion. Therefore, pretreatment is a critical step in order to disrupt the lignin-carbohydrate complex and improve the accessibility of cellulose.

Various pretreatment methods have been developed, including dilute acid, alkaline, steam explosion, organosolv and ionic liquid pretreatment. Nevertheless, these methods suffer from various drawbacks such as harsh conditions, high cost, environmental pollution and the generation of inhibitors. Moreover, ionic liquids, although effective, are expensive and toxic. Consequently, there is an urgent need to develop novel, green and cost-effective pretreatment technologies.

Deep eutectic solvents (DESs) have garnered tremendous attention as a promising alternative to ionic liquids due to their low cost, biodegradability, non-toxicity, easy preparation and tunable properties. DESs are typically formed by mixing a hydrogen bond acceptor (HBA) such as choline chloride (ChCl) with a hydrogen bond donor (HBD) such as carboxylic acids, polyols or amides. Numerous studies have demonstrated that DESs are capable of selectively removing lignin from biomass. Furthermore, the lignin extracted by DES generally has a low molecular weight and retains a high content of β-O-4 linkages, which is beneficial for downstream valorization.

In this study, we developed a novel ChCl:lactic acid DES for the pretreatment of corn stover. The effect of pretreatment temperature, time and solid loading on delignification and enzymatic digestibility was systematically investigated. The structural changes of the pretreated biomass and the extracted lignin were comprehensively characterized. Additionally, the recyclability of the DES was evaluated. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first report on the pretreatment of corn stover using ChCl:lactic acid DES at these conditions. This work not only provides a green and efficient pretreatment strategy but also offers valuable insights into the mechanism of DES delignification.

2. Materials and Methods

2.1 Materials

Corn stover was obtained from a local farm in Shandong province, China. It was air-dried, milled and sieved to 40-60 mesh. Choline chloride (98%), lactic acid (85 wt%), and all other chemicals were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich and used as received. Cellulase (Cellic CTec2) was kindly provided by Novozymes.

2.2 DES preparation and pretreatment

ChCl and lactic acid were mixed at a molar ratio of 1:2, 1:5 and 1:10 and heated at 80°C under stirring until a clear homogeneous liquid was formed. For pretreatment, 2g of corn stover was added into 20g of DES in a 50 ml round bottom flask and heated at 100, 120 and 140 °C for 1, 3 and 6 hrs. After pretreatment, 20 mL of ethanol was added and the mixture was filtered. The solid residue was washed with ethanol and water until neutral and then dried at 60 °C over night. Lignin was recovered from the filtrate by adding 100 ml water as antisolvent and centrifuged at 8000rpm for 10 minutes.

2.3 Enzymatic hydrolysis

Enzymatic hydrolysis was carried out in 50 mM citrate buffer (pH 4.8) at 50°C and 150 rpm for 72h with 2% solid loading and enzyme loading of 20 FPU/g glucan. Glucose was determined by HPLC.

2.4 Characterization

The chemical composition of raw and pretreated corn stover was determined according to NREL procedure. FTIR spectra were recorded on a Nicolet iS50 spectrometer. XRD patterns were collected on a Bruker D8 Advance diffractometer, and the crystallinity index (CrI) was calculated using the Segal method. SEM images were obtained on a Hitachi SU8010. 2D-HSQC NMR spectra of lignin were recorded on a Bruker 600 MHz in DMSO-d6. Molecular weight of lignin was determined by GPC.

3. Results and Discussion

3.1 Effect of pretreatment conditions

The effect of pretreatment conditions on delignification and glucose yield is shown in Figure 1. As can be seen from Figure 1a, delignification increased significantly with increasing temperature. At 100°C, only 45.2% of lignin was removed after 3 h, whereas at 140°C the delignification reached 78.4%. Similarly, prolonging the pretreatment time from 1 h to 6 h led to a significant increase in delignification. It is worth noting that the molar ratio of ChCl to lactic acid also had a significant impact on delignification, with 1:5 giving the best performance. This can be attributed to the optimal balance between the hydrogen bonding ability and viscosity of the DES.

Furthermore, the glucose yield after enzymatic hydrolysis showed a similar trend (Figure 1b). The untreated corn stover only gave a glucose yield of 21.3%, while the corn stover pretreated at 140°C for 3 h gave a glucose yield of 84.7%, which is a 4-fold enhancement. This remarkable improvement can be attributed to the efficient removal of lignin and hemicellulose, which significantly increased the accessibility of cellulose to enzymes. It should be noted that further increasing the pretreatment time to 6 h did not further improve the glucose yield, which may be due to the degradation of cellulose under harsh conditions. Therefore, 140°C and 3 h was selected as the optimal conditions for the subsequent experiments.

3.2 Characterization of pretreated corn stover

The chemical composition of raw and pretreated corn stover is summarized in Table 1. The raw corn stover contained 35.6% glucan, 21.2% xylan and 19.8% lignin. After DES pretreatment at the optimal conditions, the glucan content increased to 62.3%, while the xylan and lignin content decreased to 8.7% and 6.9%, respectively. This clearly indicates that the DES pretreatment selectively removed lignin and hemicellulose while preserving most of the cellulose.

FTIR spectra (Figure 2a) revealed that the intensity of the peaks at 1510 and 1600 cm-1, which are assigned to the aromatic skeletal vibrations of lignin, decreased significantly after pretreatment, confirming the removal of lignin. Moreover, the peak at 1730 cm-1 attributed to the C=O stretching of acetyl groups in hemicellulose almost disappeared, indicating the removal of hemicellulose. XRD analysis (Figure 2b) showed that the CrI increased from 42.1% to 58.6% after pretreatment, which was due to the removal of amorphous lignin and hemicellulose. SEM images (Figure 2c,d) demonstrated that the surface of the raw corn stover was smooth and compact, whereas the pretreated corn stover exhibited a rough and porous morphology, highlighting the disruption of the cell wall structure and the enhanced accessibility of cellulose.

3.3 Characterization of extracted lignin

The extracted lignin was characterized by 2D-HSQC NMR and GPC in order to gain insights into its structural features. As shown in Figure 3, the β-O-4 linkages were largely retained in the DES lignin, with a content of 38.2 per 100 aromatic units compared to 52.4 in the milled wood lignin. The molecular weight (Mw) of the DES lignin was 3450 g/mol with a polydispersity of 1.8, which is significantly lower than that of the milled wood lignin. These results indicate that the DES cleaved the ether bonds in lignin during pretreatment while avoiding excessive condensation. Consequently, the DES lignin is a promising candidate for downstream depolymerization into aromatic monomers.

3.4 Recyclability of DES

The recyclability of the DES is of paramount importance for the economic viability and sustainability of the process. After lignin precipitation, the DES was recovered by rotary evaporation to remove ethanol and water. As shown in Figure 4, the delignification and glucose yield only decreased slightly from 78.4% to 72.1% and from 84.7% to 79.3%, respectively, after five cycles, demonstrating the excellent recyclability of the DES. The slight decrease may be due to the accumulation of dissolved lignin fragments and sugar degradation products in the DES.

4. Conclusions

In summary, we have successfully developed a novel and green ChCl:lactic acid DES for the efficient pretreatment of corn stover. Under the optimal conditions (140°C, 3 h, ChCl:lactic acid = 1:5), 78.4% of lignin was removed and the glucose yield reached 84.7%. Comprehensive characterization revealed that the DES selectively removed lignin and hemicellulose, disrupted the cell wall structure and increased the cellulose accessibility. The extracted lignin retained a high content of β-O-4 linkages and had a low molecular weight, making it a promising feedstock for valorization. Moreover, the DES could be recycled for at least five times with negligible loss of performance. This work provides a green, efficient and sustainable strategy for the fractionation of lignocellulosic biomass and paves the way for the development of integrated biorefineries.
