# Advanced Functional Materials (Wiley): what the journal requires

Compiled on 10 Nov 2026 from the journal's own pages at advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/hub/journal/16163028: Author Guidelines (tabs Guide for Authors, Article Types, Editorial Policies, Data Reporting Checklists, Graphics) and Overview. Quoted text is the journal's wording. Items marked *(template)* follow the journal's Word template and published articles and were not re-read line by line; the author should open the current template to confirm them. Guidelines change: when a requirement decides whether the paper is accepted or returned, tell the author to confirm it on the live page.

## 1. Journal facts

- *Advanced Functional Materials*, abbreviated "Adv. Funct. Mater.", commonly AFM; Wiley-VCH; weekly (52 issues a year). Part of the Wiley "Advanced" family with *Advanced Materials*, *Small*, *Advanced Science*, *Advanced Materials Technologies*, *Macromolecular Rapid Communications* and others.
- Aims and scope: "reports breakthrough research in all aspects of materials science, including nanotechnology, chemistry, physics, and biology."
- Readership, as the journal lists it: "Materials scientists, chemists, physicists, ceramicists, engineers, metallurgists."
- The guide's opening instruction: "Contributors should bear in mind the interdisciplinary nature of the readership ... and, when applicable, emphasize the importance of the topic being presented to the research community, extending beyond specialists in the field."
- Submission system: "Wiley Authors" (the link "Start your submission" on the journal page). Submissions begun before 25 Nov 2025 were handled in Editorial Manager.
- Manuscripts are handled by "professional in-house editors", sent to at least two reviewers, single-anonymous review.
- The journal provides a Manuscript Preparation Checklist, a Word template and a LaTeX template on the Author Guidelines page. The author does not need the template for a first submission; mention that it exists and shows every section in place, for when they revise.

## 2. Article types and length

**Research Article** (the default):

- "Unsolicited, peer-reviewed articles that report original research. Their essential findings should be novel and should not have been published previously, and their conclusions must be adequately supported by the data."
- "A typical Research Article is around 3,000–8,000 words in length and includes 3–8 display items (figures, schemes, or tables); however, manuscripts may be of any length if the scientific content justifies it."
- Must include "1) an introduction summarizing the objectives and main conclusions of the work, 2) the main text of the article, 3) a conclusion summarizing the conclusions that can be drawn, and 4) an experimental or methods section."
- "A short abstract (200 words maximum) and 3–7 keywords."
- "A Data Availability Statement (DAS) is required." Conflicts of interest must be disclosed. An ethics statement is mandatory for work with human participants or animals.
- "The main article must stand on its own in the absence of the Supporting Information."

**Other types:**

| Type | Notes |
|---|---|
| Review | Typically 10,000–20,000 words, 5–15 display items; abstract 200 words maximum, 3–7 keywords; photo and biography (100 words maximum) of main authors; may be unsolicited, but ones "judged too specialized" are referred to sister journals or returned |
| Perspective | Any length, concise; "generally invited by the editorial office" |
| Correspondence | Comment on a paper published in the journal |

## 3. What is needed at each stage

The journal offers **Free Format submission** for new papers: "You do not need to reformat your manuscript for a new submission. However, authors are requested to follow the guidelines presented here when preparing their Revised Manuscripts."

| Stage | Needed |
|---|---|
| **First submission** | An editable manuscript file (Word, or one PDF made from LaTeX) with text, figures and tables. "All required sections should be contained in your manuscript, including abstract (which does need to be correctly styled), introduction, methods, results, and conclusions." Figures and tables with legends. References in any one consistent style. Supporting Information as a separate file. An **ORCID iD** for the submitting author. A **title page** with every co-author's affiliation and email address and these statements: data availability statement, funding statement, conflict of interest disclosure, plus ethics approval, consent and permission statements where they apply. A Data Reporting Checklist if the work is on batteries, solar cells, supercapacitors, machine learning, or biological and biomedical science. |
| **Revision** | The journal's format in §4–§7; a point-by-point response letter; one copy with changes highlighted in a different color ("Main document - tracked changes") and one clean copy. Table of Contents text and graphic. |

Two warnings from the guide that apply at first submission: "If the manuscript, figures or tables are difficult for you to read, they will also be difficult for the editors and reviewers, and the editorial office will send it back to you for revision. Your manuscript may also be sent back to you for revision if the quality of English language is poor."

Editing advice that follows: format to §4 now (it is the format of the template the journal asks authors to use), and tell the author which items are needed today and which at revision.

## 4. Manuscript components, in the journal's order

"Title – Author(s) – (Dedication) – Affiliation(s) – Keywords – Abstract – Main text [including Figures, Schemes and Tables] – (Experimental/Methods Section) – Acknowledgements – References – (Biographies) – Table of Contents text."

**Title.** "Should clearly describe the research using relevant keywords and it should avoid catchy but non-descriptive phrases." Title Case: the first letter of every word capitalized except coordinating conjunctions, articles and prepositions. No references. Avoid chemical and mathematical formulae in the title because they break database indexing. No length limit is given; keep it to what a search would need.

**Authors.** First name, middle initial(s), surname. An asterisk marks each corresponding author; no other symbols. For joint first authors add a sentence on the first page: "X.X. and Y.Y. contributed equally to this work."

**Affiliations.** A list giving, for each address, the authors there (first names as initials), department, university, city and country. An institutional or company email address is required for every corresponding author. The journal also expects the senior scientist to hold a permanent position.

**Keywords.** 3 to 7.

**Abstract.** Required. "Should not exceed 200 words and should be written in the present tense and impersonal style (i.e., avoiding we). Abbreviations must be defined when first used and the abstract should not contain any references." This is the one element the journal says must be correctly styled even in a free-format submission. "We synthesized a hydrogel that showed ..." becomes "A hydrogel is synthesized that shows ..." or, better, a sentence with the material as the subject: "The hydrogel stretches to 1200% strain and recovers ...".

**Main text.** Sections with numbered headings *(template)*: 1. Introduction; 2. Results and Discussion (with numbered subsections 2.1, 2.2); 3. Conclusion; 4. Experimental Section (the template also allows "Methods"). The Introduction should summarize "the objectives and main conclusions of the work". The Conclusion summarizes what can be concluded; it should not copy the abstract.

**Figures, schemes and tables.** Placed in-line in the main text, each with a caption, cited in numerical order. The full word "Figure" and "Table" everywhere, never "Fig.".

**Experimental Section.** After the Conclusion. Requirements that reviewers check:

- "The structure and compositions of all materials central to the manuscript must be disclosed in the main text or in Supporting Information, this includes commercial and proprietary products, pure materials, and mixtures. Manuscripts reporting results using undisclosed material compositions may not be considered for publication and may be returned without external review."
- If a supplier will not disclose what a product contains, the author's options are to obtain the composition, to replace the product with a disclosed material, or to ask the editorial office before submitting. Say this plainly; do not word around it.
- Hazardous chemicals, equipment and techniques must be emphasized.
- Quantities in mass or volume and in moles; yields as mass and percent ("109 mg, 95%").
- New compounds and materials fully characterized, with evidence of composition, structure and purity; spectra of key compounds in the Supporting Information.
- For micro- and nanostructured materials, "detailed information about both the composition and morphology".
- Measurement settings and sample dimensions reported, and any normalization stated, so that data are comparable.
- Microscopy at a magnification that shows a representative sample; high-magnification images accompanied by low-magnification ones; histograms and statistics for size distributions encouraged.
- **A subsection titled "Statistical Analysis" at the end of the Experimental Section**, describing: pre-processing of data (normalization, outliers); how data are presented (for example mean ± SD); sample size *n* for each analysis; the statistical test, whether one- or two-sided, the significance level and any post-hoc test; the software used. Figure and table legends must repeat *n*, the test, the P value and the meaning of significance symbols.
- Work with human participants, "including the testing of sensor/wearable technologies", needs a statement naming the ethics committee or its exemption, a statement that informed consent was obtained, and conformity with the Declaration of Helsinki. Animal work needs the approving body named in the Experimental Section.

**Supporting Information** *(template)*. A heading with the line "Supporting Information is available from the Wiley Online Library or from the author." The Supporting Information itself is a separate Word or PDF file carrying the paper's title and all author names, with its graphics embedded; it is published unedited, so it must be error-free. The journal's term is *Supporting* Information, items numbered Figure S1, Table S1.

**Acknowledgements.** British spelling in the heading. "All funding sources relevant to the work reported should be acknowledged, including grant numbers and names of funding bodies." Non-author contributors are named with their permission.

**Conflict of Interest.** "All authors must declare financial/commercial conflicts of interest. If the authors have no conflicts of interest, this should be stated." Standard wording in published articles: "The authors declare no conflict of interest."

**Author Contributions** *(template)*. Optional; CRediT-style sentence if included.

**Data Availability Statement.** Required. Wiley's standard wordings, of which only the author can choose the true one:

- "The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request."
- "The data that support the findings of this study are available in the supplementary material of this article."
- "The data that support the findings of this study are openly available in [repository] at [DOI or URL], reference number [number]."

The journal "expect[s] that data supporting primary research results will be archived in an appropriate public repository"; when data are shared, the statement links to the repository and the data are cited.

**References.** See §6.

**Table of Contents text.** Last item in the file. See §5.

## 5. Table of Contents (ToC) entry

"A short text and graphic should be provided for the Table of Contents."

**Text.** "Should describe the main results in 50 to 60 words. It should be written for a general audience and be written in the third person." No "we" or "our". Count the words; the window is narrow.

**Graphic.** "Should convey the main message of the article. It does not have to be a figure from the article; it can be a combination of figures or a new, original figure composed to represent the topic. The author must be the copyright holder for this figure and any images used to create it."

- Size: **55 mm wide × 50 mm high**, or **110 mm wide × 20 mm high**. As bitmaps: 650 pixels wide for the first, 1300 pixels wide for the second.
- Labels at least 10 pt. Insets discouraged.
- Must fit the research; no religious or political imagery, no stereotyping of people; permissions needed for any reused element.

Do not generate the image. Wiley states that generative AI tools should not be used for cover images and requires the author to hold copyright in the ToC figure; an AI-generated picture puts both at risk. Describe a layout the author can assemble from their own figures.

## 6. Style and references

**Language.** "Manuscripts must be written in American English and be grammatically and linguistically correct." Convert British spellings (colour, behaviour, characterise, fibre, modelling, sulphur) in the text; the heading "Acknowledgements" is the journal's own exception.

**Symbols, units and numbers.**

- SI units. A space between number and unit; units upright: "25 °C", "5 mg mL⁻¹".
- Negative exponents, not slashes: "25.8 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹", "V cm⁻¹".
- Symbols for physical quantities in italics (*T*, *E*, *σ*), units upright.
- Axis labels as "variable [units]", for example *T* [K] or Stress [MPa].
- Equations numbered and cited as "Equation (1)"; typed with an equation editor, never pasted as pictures.
- Italics for stereochemical descriptors, locants and prefixes (*cis*, *N*-methyl, *tert*-butyl) and for species names. Latin phrases such as "in situ" are **not** italicized.
- Compound numbers in bold.
- Abbreviations used sparingly and defined at first use; bp, DNA, RNA, NMR, UV–vis and wt need no definition.
- IUPAC nomenclature, American usage.
- Characterization data in the journal's compact form. The guide's examples: "¹H NMR (200 MHz, [D₈]THF, 25 °C, TMS): δ = 1.3 (q, ³J(H,H) = 8 Hz, 2 H; CH₂), 0.9 ppm (t, ³J(H,H) = 8 Hz, 3 H; CH₃)"; "IR (Nujol): ν = 1790 cm⁻¹ (C=O)"; "UV–vis (n-hexane): λmax (ε) = 320 (5000), 270 nm (12 000)".

**Citations in the text.** "Numbered citations (inline square brackets)", numbered in order of citation. *(Template: the bracketed number is set as a superscript after punctuation, as in "reported previously.[12]"; in pasted plain text keep "[12]".)*

**Reference list.**

- "Each numbered reference should contain only one literature citation, and not multiple sub-parts (a, b, c…)."
- Every entry needs: authors, journal or book title, **article title**, year, volume and issue or chapter, pages. DOI optional but "highly recommended".
- "There is no submission requirement for formatting references. If your manuscript is accepted for publication, the typesetters will update the formatting into the journal style." One consistent style is what is asked.
- The guide's examples use Chicago style with the full journal name: "List all authors, but if the number exceeds six please list three followed by 'et al.' Include the full length article title, and the full length journal name."

Format, from the guide's examples:

- Journal article: `H. Hussein, A. Damdam, L. Ren, et al., “Actuation of Mobile Microbots: A Review,” Advanced Intelligent Systems 5, no. 9 (2023): 2300168. https://doi.org/10.1002/aisy.202300168`
- Conference paper: `J. Rutten, “Solar Chromosphere Observations and Models,” in The Physics of Chromospheric Plasmas, ed. P. Heinzel, I. Dorotovic, and R. J. Rutten, ASP Conference Series 368 (2007): 27.`

Editing rule: if the author's list is already complete and consistent in another style, leave the style alone and say so; the journal accepts it. Convert a mixed list to the layout of the guide's journal-article example above. Keep each journal name and each title's capitalization as the author wrote it (the typesetters standardize these), keep "et al." where the author used it, and add a marker to any entry that lacks an article title, authors, volume or pages. Never supply missing details, expanded journal names or extra authors from memory.

## 7. Figures and tables

- Bitmap images (photographs, SEM, TEM, AFM): TIFF or PNG, at least 300 dpi. One column is 8.5 cm wide (about 1000 pixels); two columns 17.8 cm (about 2100 pixels). Above 600 dpi brings no benefit. Never enlarge a low-resolution image artificially. Avoid JPG.
- Plots, schemes and chemical structures: vector files (PDF, PS, EPS) or embedded in the Word file.
- Axis labels, symbols and scale-bar labels 10–12 pt at one-column width.
- Panel labels: **lower-case letters** (a, b, c), 12 pt sans-serif such as Arial or Helvetica, top-left corner, with good contrast.
- Insets discouraged; use multi-panel figures.
- Captions: "Figure 1." followed by the description, with panels introduced as "a) ... b) ..."; every caption with plotted data states *n* and what the error bars are.
- Tables: a brief legend; three horizontal rules only (top, under the header, bottom); footnotes as lower-case letters with a closing parenthesis, "a)"; avoid one- or two-column tables.
- Images reused from other papers need copyright permission, even from the author's own earlier papers.
- Image processing must be minimal, applied to the whole image, and disclosed.

## 8. Policies that affect the text

**Generative AI.** "If an author has used a GenAI tool to develop any portion of a manuscript, its use must be described, transparently and in detail, in the Methods section (or via a disclosure or within the Acknowledgements section, as applicable)." Wiley's AI Guidelines for Researchers give the detail. An author who uses this skill has used such a tool and should add a sentence; suggested wording in `deliverable-templates.md`. "Generative AI tools should not be used to create cover images."

**Context and prior work.** "The manuscript should represent significant progress in the field under study, as well as being presented in the context of previously published work (by the authors and other researchers) by giving due reference, especially to the latest international research."

**Related manuscripts.** The editor must be told about papers submitted, about to be submitted or in press elsewhere that bear on this one. Duplicate submission leads to rejection.

**Preprints.** Allowed on non-profit servers (ChemRxiv, bioRxiv, arXiv), original submitted version only. A thesis does not count as prior publication.

**Resubmissions.** A revised version of a paper the journal rejected may be sent only with the handling editor's consent, and the cover letter must say so and detail the changes.

**Patents.** By submitting, authors confirm that patent filings related to the content are complete.

**Appeals.** Possible only for a misunderstanding of the paper or a process problem. "Differences in opinion regarding the novelty or significance of the reported findings are not considered as grounds for appeal."

**Field-specific reporting.** Catalysis: turnover frequency or mass-specific activity, controls, stability, at least five recycling runs, benchmarking against known standards. Electrochemistry: full cell and electrode details. Computation: enough detail to reproduce, software cited. Crystal structures deposited with the CCDC and checked with CheckCIF.

## 9. Cover letter

The guide requires a cover letter only to disclose a previous rejection, but it is the natural place for what the editor must be told: related manuscripts, the preprint if any, and why the work is "significant progress" of interest "beyond specialists in the field". Keep it to half a page. Skeleton in `deliverable-templates.md`.

## 10. Why papers are declined without review

From the Peer Review Policy: "Manuscripts are rejected by the editors at this stage if they are insufficiently original, have serious scientific flaws, need extensive improvement in the language, or do not fall within the aims and scope of the journal." From the guide: undisclosed material compositions; manuscripts that are hard to read; reviews and perspectives "too specialized for the readership".

In editing terms the risks are:

- The paper describes a material and its characterization, and the function it performs is missing, minor, or demonstrated only qualitatively.
- The advance over the latest published work is not stated with numbers, or recent competing papers are not cited.
- English that needs "extensive improvement", or prose that reads as machine-written.
- A composition given as a trade name or "proprietary".
- Key numbers without replicates, error or a statistical analysis subsection.

## 11. Submission checklist

Needed for the first submission:

- [ ] Abstract: at most 200 words, present tense, impersonal (no "we"), abbreviations defined, no citations
- [ ] Introduction (objectives and main conclusions), results, conclusion, experimental or methods section all present
- [ ] Figures and tables in the text with captions; readable
- [ ] References complete (with article titles) and in one consistent style
- [ ] Supporting Information as a separate file with title and authors
- [ ] Title page: all co-authors with affiliation and email; corresponding author(s) with institutional email
- [ ] Data Availability Statement; funding statement; conflict of interest statement
- [ ] Ethics and consent statements if people (including wearable-device volunteers) or animals were involved
- [ ] All material compositions disclosed, including commercial products
- [ ] Key numbers reported with replicates and error (the Statistical Analysis subsection can be completed at revision; the numbers themselves are judged now)
- [ ] A comparison with recent published results, with references from the last two to three years
- [ ] ORCID iD for the submitting author
- [ ] Data Reporting Checklist if the field has one
- [ ] American English throughout; AI use described if a GenAI tool helped with the text

Needed by revision (prepare now where possible):

- [ ] Title in Title Case, descriptive, no formulae, no catchy phrase
- [ ] 3–7 keywords
- [ ] Sections in the template's order with numbered headings; "Figure" and "Table" spelled out
- [ ] Experimental Section ending with a "Statistical Analysis" subsection
- [ ] Citations as numbers in square brackets in order of appearance; one work per number
- [ ] Units with spaces and negative exponents; symbols italic; "in situ" not italic
- [ ] Figure panels labeled a, b, c; text 10–12 pt at one-column width; 300 dpi or better
- [ ] ToC text: 50–60 words, third person, general audience
- [ ] ToC graphic: 55 × 50 mm or 110 × 20 mm, made by the authors, labels at least 10 pt
- [ ] Permissions for any reused images
