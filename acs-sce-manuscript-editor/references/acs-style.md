# ACS style for manuscripts

The rules an ACS copyeditor applies, condensed for the style pass. Source: *The ACS Style Guide* (3rd ed., Coghill & Garson) and its successor *The ACS Guide to Scholarly Communication* (online, 2020–), plus ACS journal author guidelines. Where the current guide relaxed a rule (2025), that is noted; when in doubt, be consistent within the paper, which is what the guide itself asks for.

## 1. Numbers and units

- Numerals with every unit of time or measure, with a space: `6 min`, `25 mL`, `0.30 g`, `273 K`, `125 V/s`. Exceptions with no space: `50%`, `$5`, angular `180°`. Temperature: `180 °C` (space before °, none between ° and C).
- Words for cardinal numbers below 10 when no unit follows (`three flasks`, `30 flasks`); numerals for everything in a series that contains a number ≥ 10 (`5, 8, and 12 experiments`); numerals in mathematical senses (`a factor of 4`, `2-fold`, `3 times the risk`) but words otherwise (`rinsed four times`, `twofold`).
- Never start a sentence with a numeral; recast (`Acetone (25 mL) was added`) rather than spelling out.
- Units are never pluralized: `50 mg`, `3 mol`. Time: `h`, `min`, `s` (`3.5 h`, not `3½ h`, not `hrs`). Spell out units not preceded by a number (`several milligrams`) except in table headings and axis labels.
- Compound units: centered dot or space for multiplication, slash or negative exponent for division, parentheses after a slash: `W m⁻¹ K⁻¹`, `W/(m·K)`, `dm³ mol⁻¹ s⁻¹`. Pick one convention and hold it. `counts/s` not cps; `s⁻¹` or `Hz` not cycles per second.
- Percent-type units take a space: `wt %`, `mol %`, `vol %`; `v/v`, `w/w`. Plain percent closes up: `50%`.
- Decimal point is a period; leading zero always (`0.25`); no trailing period (`78`). Trailing zeros only when they represent precision: `27.0 °C` and `27 °C` are different claims. Keep significant figures consistent with the measurement; a yield of `84.73%` from a single HPLC run overclaims.
- Very large or small numbers: scientific notation or a prefix, avoiding more than four digits: `1.2 × 10⁶ s`, `58 µM` not `5.8 × 10⁻⁵ M`, `42.3 L` not `42,300 mL`.
- Spaces around operators used as verbs: `3.24 ± 0.01`, `Tg = 176 °C`; no space around a slash, ratio colon, or in sub/superscripts. In running text, write "when the temperature is 50 °C", not "temperature = 50 °C".
- Ratios: `1:10`, `a 1:1 (v/v) mixture`. Ratio is a colon or a slash, never an en dash. Mixtures take a slash or an en dash, never a colon (`hexane–benzene`, `hexane/benzene`).

## 2. Ranges, dashes, operators

- En dash for "to/through": `12–20 months`, `100–125 °C`, `Figures 1–4`, `refs 3–5`, `pp 165–172`. Not with "from" or "between" (`from 500 to 600 mL`, `between 7 and 10 days`) and not when a number is negative or carries a symbol (`−20 to +120 K`, `<5 to 15 mg`).
- En dash between equal-weight terms and two-name eponyms: `acid–base titration`, `structure–activity relationship`, `Diels–Alder reaction`, `Beer–Lambert law`, `choline chloride–lactic acid DES`. Hyphen for compound modifiers (`bio-based`, `two-step`, `blue-green`).
- Em dashes only to set off words that would be misunderstood otherwise. Almost never in a chemistry paper; they also read as generated prose.
- Multiplication sign `×` with spaces: `5 × 10³`; never the letter x.
- Minus sign is `−` (U+2212), not a hyphen, in negative numbers and exponents: `−20 °C`, `cm⁻¹`.

## 3. Chemical names and formulas

- Systematic IUPAC names at first mention; trivial names and abbreviations afterwards, one abbreviation per substance (not both ACN and MeCN).
- Italic element symbols that mark an attachment site: `N-ethylaniline`, `O,O,S-triethyl phosphorodithioate`; roman when naming a reaction type: `N-acetylation`. Italic indicated hydrogen: `1H-pyrrole`.
- Italic, hyphenated, never capitalized (even at sentence start or in a title): *cis*, *trans*, *tert*, *sec*, *o*, *m*, *p*, *n*, *sym*, *vic*, *gem*, *endo*, *exo*, *syn*, *anti*, *meso*, *erythro*, *threo*, *fac*, *mer*; in parentheses: (*E*), (*Z*), (*R*), (*S*). Closed-up roman prefixes: cyclohexane, isopropyl, neopentane.
- Greek letters as letters: `α-amino acid`, `β-O-4 linkage`, `β-naphthol`. Small caps for D/L configuration: `ᴅ-glucose`, `ʟ-lactic acid` (if the output medium cannot render small caps, plain D/L with a note).
- Charges: right superscript, number before sign, sign alone for unit charge: `Ca²⁺`, `NO₃⁻`, `PO₄³⁻`; never `Hg⁺⁺`.
- Oxidation state: `iron(III) nitrate`, `nickel(II)` closed up; or superscript roman in formulas.
- Isotopes: left superscript on the element (`¹³C NMR`), labeled compounds with brackets closed to the name (`[2-¹⁴C]leucine`); deuterated solvents `DMSO-*d*₆`, `CDCl₃`.
- Compound labels in text are bold numerals (`compound **3**`); keep compound numbering separate from equation and scheme numbering.
- Catalyst on support: `Pd/C`, `5 wt % Pt/Al₂O₃` (universal ACS-journal practice).
- Solvent water of hydration, formulas with subscripts: `CuSO₄·5H₂O`, `H₂SO₄`, `Na₂CO₃`.

## 4. Abbreviations

- Define at first use in the abstract, and again at first use in the main text: `deep eutectic solvent (DES)`. Then use only the abbreviation. Do not define something you use once.
- No abbreviations in the title unless universally known; no separate abbreviation list in ACS journals.
- Need no definition: NMR, IR, UV, DNA, RNA, mp, bp, eq, equiv, ref, et al., e.g., i.e., vs, wt, vol, M, mM, pH. Common technique acronyms (FTIR, XRD, SEM, TEM, HPLC, GC, MS, TGA, DSC, GPC, BET, XPS) are accepted without definition by most ACS journals in practice, but defining them costs one parenthesis and removes a reviewer comment; define them in a materials paper.
- `e.g.`, `i.e.`, `vs.`, `etc.` in parentheses, captions, and tables; in running text write "for example", "that is", "versus".
- Define nonstandard abbreviations in table footnotes even if defined in the text.

## 5. Tense, voice, person

- Past tense for what was done, by you or by others: "The mixture was heated", "Smith reported", "We found that". Present tense for facts and for what figures and tables show: "Lignin is the largest renewable source of aromatics", "Figure 2 shows". Present or past for interpretation, consistently within the paper: "These results indicate" / "The data suggest".
- Active voice when it is shorter and clearer; passive when the agent is unknown or unimportant (most of Methods). ACS explicitly allows first person "to express a purpose or a decision": "We chose 140 °C because...", "We attribute the drop to...". Avoid "we believe", "we feel", and unsupported opinion.
- Dangling modifiers are the usual cost of forced passive: "Using the procedure described previously, the partition function can be evaluated" → "Using the procedure described previously, we evaluated the partition function".
- Contractions are not used. Split infinitives are fine when they avoid ambiguity.

## 7. Title and abstract

- Title: specific terms ("a vanadium–iron alloy", not "a magnetic alloy"), no "study of", "on the", "use of", "investigation into", "towards"; no leading "The"; no abbreviations, formulas, trade names; no "novel", "new", "first", "rapid", "highly efficient", "green", "sustainable" as adjectives. Two or three words is too vague; more than ~15 is too long. A colon subtitle is fine when it adds a result; ": A Sustainable Approach" adds nothing.
- Abstract: purpose, approach, principal quantitative findings, main conclusion; 150–200 words for this journal (the general ACS guide says 80–250 depending on the journal); no citations, no figure/table/section references, no displayed equations or structures; abbreviations defined at first use; no evaluation beyond what the paper supports ("holds great promise" is evaluation); include chemical safety information if applicable.
- Introduction: state the problem in the first sentences; cite the truly pertinent literature rather than surveying the field; end by stating how this work differs from what is published.

## 8. Figures, tables, schemes, charts, equations

- Figure captions: figure number plus a brief, informative description in non-sentence form, understandable without the text, consistent wording across related figures, symbol key in the caption when there are more than four or five symbols. `Figure 3. Delignification (a) and glucose yield (b) of corn stover after ChCl–lactic acid DES pretreatment at 100–140 °C for 1–6 h. Error bars: SD, n = 3.`
- Table titles above the table, non-sentence, Title Case, details in lettered footnotes (a, b, c), every column with a heading that is not just a unit, nonstandard abbreviations defined in footnotes. Numbers in a column aligned on the decimal, same number of decimals.
- Scheme = a process or reaction sequence ("schemes show action"); Chart = a group of structures; Figure = graphs, spectra, images. Each has its own numbering. Capitalize Figure/Table/Scheme/Chart only when followed by a number. Cite every one in the text, in numerical order, before or at the paragraph that discusses it.
- Displayed equations numbered flush right in parentheses, cited as `eq 3`, `eqs 3 and 4`; "Equation 3" only at sentence start; no colon automatically introducing an equation; variables italic, units and chemical symbols roman.
- Every value that appears in a figure and in the text must match to the decimal.
- ACS SCE TOC graphic specifics are in `journal-requirements.md`.

## 9. References

Numbered in order of first citation; in-text superscript numerals after punctuation (`...as reported.¹²` or per the journal's template). Cite each reference by the same number every time. Article titles required. All authors listed (the current ACS guide permits "et al." after the tenth author; the older rule listed all). Year bold, journal abbreviation and volume italic, issue optional in parentheses, DOI at the end. Journal abbreviations per CASSI; the 2025 guide allows full titles if used consistently, but ACS journals' own templates still use abbreviations.

Converting references: keep every element the author gave (authors, title, year, volume, pages); reorder and repunctuate; do not invent a DOI, issue, or page range you cannot derive from the draft. Mark unverifiable entries `[AUTHOR: verify]`. A reference you cannot match to a real article by its own details is a red flag (hallucinated citations are now the most-checked AI tell at major publishers) and must be queried, not quietly fixed.

## 10. Quick pass checklist

1. Every number-unit pair has a space (except %, °, $); `°C` spaced correctly; no `hrs`, `mins`, `secs`, `ml`, `ug`.
2. Ranges use en dashes; negatives and exponents use the minus sign; `×` for multiplication.
3. Subscripts and superscripts restored in every formula and unit (H₂SO₄, cm⁻¹, 10³, Ca²⁺).
4. Every abbreviation defined once in the abstract and once in the text; none in the title.
5. Stereo and locant prefixes italic and lowercase; Greek letters as letters; compound labels bold.
6. Tense: Methods past; figure callouts present; interpretation consistent.
7. Usage table above applied (data are; to; because; compare with; respectively only when pairing; no "novel/first/facile/significant-without-test").
8. Figures, tables, schemes cited in order; captions self-contained; numbers match the text.
9. Reference list in ACS format, all in one style, DOIs where derivable, order of first citation.
10. No em dashes, no contractions, no "recently", no "and/or".
