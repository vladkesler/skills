# Matter (Cell Press): what the journal requires

Compiled on 10 Nov 2026 from the journal's own pages: Information for authors (cell.com/matter/information-for-authors) and its sub-pages Article types, Submit your manuscript, Revise your manuscript, Final submission and Journal policies; Aims and scope (cell.com/matter/aims); the MAP scale page (cell.com/matter/map-scale); and the Cell Press figure guidelines (cell.com/information-for-authors/figure-guidelines). Quoted text is the journal's wording. Items marked *(unconfirmed)* come from secondary sources because the official file could not be read. Guidelines change: when a requirement decides whether the paper is accepted or returned, tell the author to confirm it on the live page.

## 1. Journal facts

- *Matter*, a Cell Press journal, "a sister journal to Cell", monthly. Sister titles in the physical sciences: *Chem*, *Joule*.
- Submission system: Editorial Manager (editorialmanager.com/matter). Journal office: matter@cell.com. Editor-in-chief: Steve Cranford.
- Scope: "the general field of materials science, from nano to macro, fundamentals to application." A key focus is "transformative research, related to fundamental structure, synthesis and assembly, properties and performance of emerging material systems, as well as novel characterization methods." Any state, scale or composition of matter, experimental or theoretical.
- The journal tags every research article with material type, scale of interest and stage of development (the MAP scale, §5.2).
- Presubmission inquiry: email matter@cell.com with "a title, an abstract, and an explanation of why your paper is significant and broadly interesting"; the journal answers in a few business days. Worth suggesting when fit is uncertain.
- The page reported a median of 7 days to the first editorial decision (Nov 2026).

## 2. Article types and length

**Research article** (the default; what authors submit unsolicited):

- "Submitted articles must report a new concept or discovery that will be of interest to the general materials community. Unexpected or unusual results of broad appeal may also be of interest."
- "Submissions deemed to be routine, derivative, incremental, or excellent yet not of general interest will not be considered."
- "Articles should highlight the significance, originality, and rigor of the research so that the potential scientific impact is obvious to the broad readership of Matter."
- Length: "no rigid restrictions on page length, total character count, number of figures, or number of references." Typical: about 5,000–7,000 words. Shorter letter-like reports of 2,000–4,000 words are considered "if the length is justified by the findings."
- "Only key experimental procedures necessary for the understanding of the research should be included in the main body of text." The rest goes in the supplemental information, with enough detail for reproduction.

**Other types** (mostly commissioned; tell the author to email the journal before writing one):

| Type | Limits |
|---|---|
| Review | 8,000–10,000 words, about 12–15 figures, no more than 150 references; tutorial, broad and balanced; Progress and potential box as five key points |
| Perspective | 2,000–4,000 words, 2–5 figures, 20–60 references; one-page proposal to matter@cell.com |
| Preview | 1,500 words, 1 figure, 10 references |
| Matter of Opinion | 2,000 words, 2 figures, 8 references |
| Backstory | 1,000 words, 1 figure, 5 references; by invitation after acceptance |

## 3. What is needed at each stage

This matters for a nervous author: most formatting rules do **not** apply to the first submission. The journal says so: "For initial submissions, your manuscript does not need to follow a specific format."

| Stage | Needed |
|---|---|
| **First submission** | Title; author list, affiliations and contact details; summary; "sufficient experimental information for us to interpret the results in context"; written in English. Cover letter. Completed **declaration of interests form** (a PDF linked on the Information for authors page, in the part "Submit your manuscript" under "Other files"; required). A Progress and potential statement and a proposed MAP level with a short justification (§5). The AI-use declaration if AI tools helped prepare the text (§8). Battery, photovoltaic or catalysis checklist if the paper is in one of those fields. One PDF or a Word file; figures may sit in the text; keep figure files small (1–2 MB) and the whole submission under 20 MB. |
| **Revision** (after review) | The full format in §4, in order; a new cover letter; a point-by-point response to reviewers. |
| **Final files** (before acceptance) | Editable main text (Word preferred); each main figure as its own high-resolution file; graphical abstract; a separate Word file holding Highlights and the eTOC blurb; supplemental information; the declaration of interests form again. |

Editing advice that follows from this: put the paper into the §4 format now, because it costs the author nothing and the paper then looks at home in the journal, but tell the author honestly which items the journal needs today and which it will ask for later.

## 4. Manuscript components, in the journal's order

Title → Author list → Affiliations and footnotes → Contact information → Summary → Keywords → Introduction → Results → Discussion → Methods → Resource availability → Acknowledgments → Author contributions → Declaration of interests → Declaration of generative AI and AI-assisted technologies → Supplemental information → Figure titles and legends → Tables → References.

**Title.** "Concise, around 10–12 words (no more than 145 characters total, including spaces)"; no jargon or uncommon abbreviations; should "capture the broad conceptual significance of the work." Count the characters.

**Author list.** Full first and last names (middle initials are fine). All authors must also be entered in Editorial Manager in the same order. Author changes after submission need a form and every author's consent, so ask the author to check the list now.

**Affiliations and footnotes.** Numbered footnotes; each affiliation gives department, institution, city, state or region, country. Further numbered footnotes for present address, equal contribution ("These authors contributed equally"), senior author. The last footnote names the **lead contact** (required).

**Contact information.** Exactly one lead contact, who must also be a corresponding author. Corresponding authors are marked with an asterisk (`*` first, `**` second, `***` third). After the footnotes: `*Correspondence: name@institution.edu`, one email per corresponding author.

**Summary** (the journal's word for the abstract). "A single paragraph of no more than 150 words" containing the background or purpose, the principal results and approach and major conclusions, and "an indication of the broader significance of the work." No reference citations; avoid non-standard abbreviations. "We discourage novelty claims (e.g., use of the word 'novel' and 'first') because they are overused, tend not to add meaning, and are difficult to verify."

**Keywords.** Up to 10, separated by commas, after the summary.

**Introduction.** Succinct; gives the background a reader needs to understand the motivation; "end with a brief statement of what has been achieved." No subheadings.

**Results.** Divided into subsections with "short, informative subheadings" that state a finding. The journal's example: instead of "Analysis of factors X and Y using approach Q", write "Factor X requires factor Y to function in process Z". For a materials paper: "Hydrogen bonding between lignin and PVA doubles the toughness", not "Mechanical properties". Reuse the same wording in the figure titles.

**Discussion.** "Should not simply repeat what has been described in the results section. Rather, it should explain the implications of the work and place them into a broader context." It may be combined with the results as "Results and discussion" when that suits the paper. There is no separate Conclusions section in the journal's list; a closing paragraph of the Discussion does that job.

**Methods.** (Older Matter papers call this "Experimental procedures".) In the main text, after the Discussion and before Resource availability; enough detail to reproduce the work. If details are in the supplement, the Methods must contain this sentence: "Further details regarding the methods can be found in the supplemental information."

**Resource availability.** Required for research articles, with three subsections under that heading:

- *Lead contact*: "Requests for further information and resources should be directed to and will be fulfilled by the lead contact, [Name] ([email])."
- *Materials availability*: required even when nothing new was made. Typical wordings: "This study did not generate new unique reagents." / "All unique/stable reagents generated in this study are available from the lead contact without restriction." / "... with a completed materials transfer agreement."
- *Data and code availability*: three parts, all required. (1) A data statement, for example "[Type of] data have been deposited at [repository] and are publicly available as of the date of publication at [DOI]." or "All data reported in this paper will be shared by the lead contact upon request." (2) A code statement, for example "This paper does not report original code." or "All original code has been deposited at [repository] and is publicly available at [DOI] as of the date of publication." (3) This fixed sentence: "Any additional information required to reanalyze the data reported in this paper is available from the lead contact upon request."

Only the author knows which wording is true. Insert the section with `[AUTHOR: ...]` choices; never pick one for them.

**Acknowledgments.** Strongly encouraged: funding with grant numbers, non-author contributors, core facilities.

**Author contributions.** Required. Initials, CRediT terms encouraged: "Conceptualization, A.B.; methodology, C.D.; investigation, A.B. and E.F.; writing – original draft, A.B.; supervision, C.D."

**Declaration of interests.** Required, and it must match the form. If nothing to declare: "The authors declare no competing interests." Funding does not go here.

**Declaration of generative AI and AI-assisted technologies in the manuscript preparation process.** Required when such tools were used; see §8 for the wording.

**Supplemental information.** A short index under that heading: "Document S1. Supplemental methods and Figures S1–S6", then standalone files such as "Data S1. NMR spectra". The journal's term is *supplemental* information, with items numbered Figure S1, Table S1.

**Figure titles and legends.** For first submission the figures and legends may sit in the text near where they are cited. At acceptance the legends are collected in one section and figures are uploaded separately. Each figure gets a brief title that describes the whole figure without citing panels, followed by the legend describing each panel. Legends must define all measures and error bars ("Data are represented as mean ± SD"), state the number of replicates, name any statistical test and define significance symbols, define scale bars, and point to related supplemental items ("See also Figure S1"). Figures are cited in numerical order.

**Tables.** After the figure legends; made with Word's table tool; black and white; one value per cell; "Table 1", never "Table 1A"; a title is required; footnotes use superscript lowercase letters; citations inside tables are written as "Smith et al.¹".

**References.** See §6.

## 5. Matter-only pieces

### 5.1 Progress and potential

A boxed statement published with every Matter article, written for non-experts. "On submission, all authors are required to submit a scientific abstract along with a more general future-impact statement" answering:

- Why is the research important?
- What are the longer-term ambitions of the research?
- Does the research have the potential to make an impact on society in the short or long term? In what field, for what application?

Form: one or two paragraphs. **Length: the two official pages disagree.** The Article types page says "a maximum of 300 words and 1,000 characters, including spaces"; the Final submission page says "a maximum of 300 words". Draft it to fit within 1,000 characters including spaces, which satisfies both, and tell the author about the discrepancy in case they want the longer form.

It "may also be speculative in nature" but must be "substantiated by the key results presented in the article", with technical jargon minimized. This is the one place in the paper where outlook is invited; it still has to follow from the paper's own numbers.

### 5.2 MAP scale (Material Advancement Progression)

The author proposes a level, with a short justification, at submission; editors and reviewers confirm it. It is "a variation of technology readiness levels" and it is "merely an assessment of current state", not a ranking of importance.

| Level | Name | What qualifies |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Discovery | A previously undocumented material response or phenomenon; a new structure or composition without precedent; a new characterization approach based on new physics. Not available if the material existed before or the behavior was seen in a similar system. |
| 2 | Benchmark | Properties or structure "quantified, observed, explained for the first time for a particular system, but not intrinsically novel": first measurements on a new material or composite, first assembly of two well-known components. Not available if earlier published values can serve as the benchmark. |
| 3 | Understanding | Properties, structures and behaviors "quantified and extended from prior reports", limits of performance mapped, dependency studies (temperature, humidity, composition). No application needed. |
| 4 | Demonstrate | Properties "quantified and extended with designed intent; proof-of-concept systems"; proof of function. Need not be application-ready. |
| 5 | Improvement | Response exploited or designed for a specific application; the combination, architecture or use case is new while the underlying science is understood; operating conditions considered. |
| 6 | Development | Demonstrated for a specific application; application-ready. |

Rules for choosing: when a paper spans levels, "the lowest applicable MAP scale value will be prioritized" together with the paper's primary focus. The journal expects mostly low numbers ("very rarely a 5 or 6"). "The only requirement is conceptual novelty, which can occur at any MAP value." Most bio-based and polymer materials papers that show a material performing a function land at 3 or 4.

### 5.3 Highlights

- Up to four bullet points conveying the core findings.
- Each no more than **85 characters including spaces**. Count them; do not estimate.
- No abbreviations.

### 5.4 eTOC blurb ("In brief")

A short summary shown on the article's cover page and in the journal's email alerts. It should present the context and significance of the findings, be accessible to scientists outside the field, be written in the third person (no "we" or "I"), and be **50 words or fewer, in two sentences**.

Highlights and the eTOC blurb go together in one Word file, separate from the manuscript, at the final-files stage. Draft them at first submission anyway: they force the paper's message into the open, and the author will need them.

### 5.5 Graphical abstract

Required for Matter at the final-files stage. "A single-panel, square image that is designed to give readers an immediate understanding of the take-home message of the paper." The Cell Press graphical abstract guide gives the technical specification; secondary sources report 1200 × 1200 pixels at 300 dpi, Arial 12–16 pt, TIFF, PDF or JPG *(unconfirmed; the author should open the guide linked from the Final submission page)*.

"General-purpose generative AI image tools may not be used in creation of graphical abstracts." Do not generate one. Describe a layout the author can draw: what goes on the left (the starting material or problem), the middle (what was done), the right (the result with its one key number).

## 6. Style and references

**Writing.** "Cell Press journals are read by audiences across multiple disciplines. Therefore, it is essential that all manuscripts be accessible to a broad readership interested in diverse scientific fields."

- US spelling and number punctuation.
- Abbreviations kept to a minimum, avoided in the title, non-standard ones defined at first use.
- IUPAC nomenclature where possible; common names are acceptable for compounds.
- Trade names capitalized, no ™ or ® symbols.
- Footnotes only in the author list and in tables.
- "We discourage the use of novelty claims (e.g., use of the words 'novel' and 'first')."
- Equations editable (Word's equation tool), referred to as "Equation 1"; variables in italics.

**Citations in the text.** Superscript numbers in order of first citation: "Multiple reports support this observation¹,²" or "This observation is supported by Smith et al.¹". If the draft uses bracketed numbers or author–year citations, convert the form and keep the numbering.

**Reference list.**

- Numbered in order of first citation; one work per number, no parts a, b, c.
- "et al." only after 10 author names.
- Only published or in-press work; preprints are allowed with a DOI; unpublished data, personal communications and conference abstracts are cited in the text only.
- DOIs encouraged. Data sets and code that were used must be cited.
- References cited only in the supplemental information stay in the supplemental information.

Format, from the journal's examples:

- Journal article: `Sondheimer, N., and Lindquist, S. (2000). Rnq1: an epigenetic modifier of protein function in yeast. Mol. Cell 5, 163–172. 10.1016/S1097-2765(00)80412-8.`
- Book chapter: `King, S.M. (2003). Dynein motors: Structure, mechanochemistry and regulation. In Molecular Motors, M. Schliwa, ed. (Wiley-VCH Verlag GmbH), pp. 45–78.`
- Book: `Cowan, W.M., Jessell, T.M., and Zipursky, S.L. (1997). Molecular and Cellular Approaches to Neural Development (Oxford University Press).`
- Preprint: `De Virgilio, C., Hatakeyama, R., Péli-Gulli, M.-P., Hu, Z., Jaquenoud, M., Osuna, G.M.G., Sardu, A., and Dengjel, J. (2018). Spatially distinct pools of TORC1 balance protein homeostasis. Preprint at Mendeley Data, 10.17632/m9s42s94fc.1.`
- Data or code: `Gerczuk, M. (2023). HyperPersonalisation. Zenodo. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.8328092.`
- Patent: `Smith, J.C. (2016). Process for batch culturing. US patent US9340678 B2, filed June 14, 2011, and granted May 17, 2016.`

Pattern for articles: Surname, Initials., Surname, Initials., and Surname, Initials. (Year). Title in sentence case. Abbreviated Journal Name volume, pages. DOI. Reformat what the author supplied. Keep each journal name as the author wrote it if you are not certain of the standard abbreviation, keep "et al." where the author used it, and add a marker to any entry that lacks a title, authors, volume or pages. Never fill in missing details from memory.

## 7. Figures and tables

For first submission any format is accepted. For production: one file per figure with all panels, at most 16.5 × 20 cm; widths 8.5 cm (one column), 11.4 cm (1.5 columns) or 17.4 cm (full width); TIFF or PDF preferred; at most 20 MB each.

Artwork rules the author should know now, because redrawing later is painful: Arial only; panels labeled with **capital letters** (A, B, C); text about 6–8 pt at print size; at least 300 dpi for color and grayscale, 1,000 dpi for line art; RGB color; red and green not used together; line weights 0.5–1.5 pt; no reference numbers inside artwork; scale bar on every microscopy image.

Data presentation: the journal recommends plotting individual data points in addition to the mean ± error, and "articles must include replicate results with means and standard deviations" where applicable. Figures reused from other publications need written permission and a credit line; this can take weeks.

## 8. Policies that affect the text

**Generative AI in writing** (Elsevier policy, applied by Cell Press). Authors may use AI tools for tasks "such as content organization and improving language and readability" with human oversight, and remain accountable for the content. Use "must be declared by adding a statement at the end of the manuscript when the paper is first submitted", in a new section after the Declaration of interests, titled *Declaration of generative AI and AI-assisted technologies in the manuscript preparation process*:

> During the preparation of this work, the author(s) used [NAME TOOL / SERVICE] in order to [REASON]. After using this tool/service, the author(s) reviewed and edited the content as needed and take(s) full responsibility for the content of the published article.

The declaration "does not apply to the use of basic tools, such as tools used to check grammar, spelling, and references." An assistant rewriting sentences is more than that, so an author who uses this skill should include the statement. AI tools cannot be listed as authors.

**Generative AI in images.** Allowed for explanatory illustrations if declared in the figure legend and the AI statement. Not allowed to create or alter research or data images, for cover art, or (general-purpose image tools) for graphical abstracts.

**Preprints.** Allowed on established servers (arXiv, ChemRxiv, bioRxiv, SSRN), original submitted version only.

**Related manuscripts.** Any related paper in preparation, submitted or in press must be mentioned in the cover letter and uploaded as a related file; failing to do so "may be grounds for rejection."

**Originality.** Not published and not under consideration elsewhere; every author approves. Submissions are checked with Crossref Similarity Check.

**Competing interests.** The form plus the in-text section; thresholds include payments above $10,000 a year, 5% ownership, company research funding, patents, and membership of the journal's advisory board.

**New chemical compounds.** Exact structure with supporting data (typically ¹H and ¹³C NMR plus HRMS); synthesis described in the methods; crystal structures deposited in the CSD with the identifier in the data availability statement.

**Humans and animals.** Ethics committee, approval number and consent stated in the Methods. This includes volunteers wearing a sensor or testing a device.

**Images.** Adjustments applied to the whole image and disclosed; original unprocessed data kept available; accepted papers are screened for image irregularities.

## 9. Cover letter

Seen only by editors. The journal lists what it should explain:

1. "What was previously known (the background)"
2. "The conceptual advance provided by your work"
3. "The significance of the work and its potential impact"
4. Whether the paper is part of a consortium

It may also suggest or exclude reviewers, mention related work, time constraints or competition, and note earlier contact with an editor. Add the related-manuscript disclosure (§8) and any restriction on sharing materials. Skeleton in `deliverable-templates.md`.

## 10. Why papers are declined without review

The journal's own description of what it will not consider: work that is "routine, derivative, incremental, or excellent yet not of general interest." In editing terms the risks are:

- The advance is stated for specialists only; a materials scientist from another subfield cannot tell why it matters.
- The paper reports an improved number with no new concept, mechanism or understanding behind it.
- The title and summary describe what was made, and never say what was learned.
- Significance rests on adjectives ("novel", "first", "unprecedented") that the journal says it discourages.
- No replicates or statistics behind the key numbers.

## 11. Submission checklist

Needed for the first submission:

- [ ] Title around 10–12 words, at most 145 characters with spaces, no uncommon abbreviations, states the conceptual significance
- [ ] Authors, affiliations, corresponding author(s) with email, one lead contact
- [ ] Summary: one paragraph, at most 150 words, no citations, no "novel" or "first", ends on the broader significance
- [ ] Enough experimental detail to judge the results; replicates with mean ± SD
- [ ] Progress and potential statement (within 1,000 characters to be safe; at most 300 words)
- [ ] Proposed MAP level with one or two sentences of justification
- [ ] Cover letter: background, conceptual advance, significance, related manuscripts
- [ ] Declaration of interests form (PDF) completed
- [ ] AI-use declaration section, if AI tools helped write or edit the text
- [ ] Battery, photovoltaic or catalysis checklist, if relevant
- [ ] One PDF or Word file; figures 1–2 MB each; total under 20 MB

Needed by revision or acceptance (prepare now where possible):

- [ ] Sections in the journal's order; Introduction without subheadings; Results subheadings that state findings; Discussion that interprets and does not repeat
- [ ] Methods in the main text, with the supplemental-information sentence if details are there
- [ ] Resource availability with lead contact, materials availability, data and code availability (three parts)
- [ ] Acknowledgments with funding; Author contributions; Declaration of interests matching the form
- [ ] Supplemental information index (Document S1 ...)
- [ ] Figure titles and legends with replicates, error-bar definitions, tests, scale bars
- [ ] References numbered in citation order, superscript in text, Cell Press format, DOIs, one work per number
- [ ] Up to 10 keywords
- [ ] Highlights: at most four, each at most 85 characters with spaces, no abbreviations
- [ ] eTOC blurb: at most 50 words, two sentences, third person
- [ ] Graphical abstract: square, single panel, drawn by the authors, no AI image generator
- [ ] Figure files: Arial, capital panel letters, 300 dpi or better, permissions for reused images
